Build Procedure
1
Print the four quarter-segments

Load each 8hz_coil_quarter_N.stl into PrusaSlicer. Orient each piece with the flat cut face down on the bed. Each quarter is approximately 158 × 158 mm, fitting well within the MK4S build area.

Settings: 0.2 mm layer height, 3 perimeters, 20% gyroid infill, no supports needed. Print time is roughly 2.5 hours per segment.

Material: PLA is fine for indoor use. For outdoor installations, use PETG — it handles UV and humidity better.
2
Assemble the circular former

Dry-fit all four quarters into a complete ring. The dovetail tabs on each cut face interlock with the flat face of the adjacent segment. Check alignment — the flanges should form continuous ridges.

Apply CA glue to each joint, hold for 30 seconds. For a stronger bond, run a bead of epoxy along the inner seam after the CA sets. The assembled ring should be 315 mm in diameter, 60 mm long.

3
Wind the first layer (100 turns)

Anchor the wire start through a small hole or tape it to the flange. Leave a 15 cm tail for later connection. Wind 100 turns in a single layer, keeping the wire taut and each turn snug against the previous one.

The winding channel between flanges is 50 mm wide. At 0.51 mm wire diameter, 100 turns fills 51 mm — a tight but manageable fit. Aim for neat, even coverage.

Tension: Moderate and consistent. Too loose and turns shift; too tight and you risk cracking the printed flanges. A hand-crank coil winder makes this much easier.
4
Complete all 10 layers

After each layer of 100 turns, apply a strip of Kapton tape over the winding to prevent the next layer from slipping into gaps. Then wind the next 100 turns on top.

Repeat for all 10 layers × 100 turns = 1,000 turns total. The finished winding stack is approximately 5 mm deep (10 layers × 0.51 mm). Leave a 15 cm tail at the finish.

Total wire used: approximately 942 metres. At ~30 seconds per turn, expect 8–10 hours of winding time.

Don't rush layers 8–10. The winding gets harder as it builds up — the channel gets tighter and wire tension becomes more critical. Take breaks.
5
Seal the winding

Wrap the finished coil with a final layer of Kapton tape. For extra security, brush on a thin coat of polyurethane varnish over the outer winding — this locks the turns in place and prevents vibration noise.

Allow to dry fully (24 hours) before electrical connection.

6
Connect the tuning capacitor

Solder or screw the two wire tails to a terminal block. Connect the 860 µF capacitor in parallel across the coil terminals. This tunes the LC circuit to resonate at exactly 7.83 Hz:

f = 1 / (2π√(LC))   →   C = 860 µF

If you can't find an 860 µF cap, combine values in parallel: 680 + 180 = 860 µF, or 470 + 390 = 860 µF. Film capacitors are preferred for low loss; electrolytics work but add ESR.

7
Connect the signal chain

The coil output is extremely weak (picovolts to nanovolts). You need a low-noise preamplifier with:

• Gain: 60–80 dB (1,000–10,000×)
• Input noise: < 1 nV/√Hz
• Bandwidth: 1–50 Hz band-pass
• Good candidates: INA217, AD797, OPA1612

Feed the amplified signal into a 24-bit ADC (ADS1256 or similar) sampling at 100–200 Hz, connected to a Raspberry Pi or similar logger.

50/60 Hz rejection: Add a twin-T notch filter at your local mains frequency. Schumann signals live at 7.83, 14.3, 20.8, 27.3, 33.8 Hz — well below mains, but the mains fundamental will saturate your preamp without a notch.
8
Position and orient the antenna

The magnetic loop is sensitive along the axis perpendicular to the coil plane. For Schumann resonances, orient the coil vertically with its axis pointing magnetic North–South.

Place the antenna as far as possible from:

• Power lines and transformers (50/60 Hz EMI)
• Electric motors, inverters, switching supplies
• Computer monitors and WiFi routers
• Metal structures (they distort the field)

Ideally, run the antenna outdoors in a rural or suburban location. Even a back garden 30+ metres from the house significantly reduces interference.
